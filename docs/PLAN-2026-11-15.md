### 2.8 Second-pass corrections (15 Nov, evening)

1. **One question, not two.** The separate "Has the deadline passed?" step is retired. The first date the student already enters now decides the route: a past date sets `after_deadline`, today or later sets `before_deadline`, and an undecided date stays `unknown`. Presets that carry no date keep the timing they were built with. Twelve regression assertions cover those four cases, and a source check confirms no sequence still asks the timing question.
2. **Readable default type.** The root size moved from 16px to 17px, and body copy that had drifted to 0.68–0.78rem is lifted to 0.9rem (about 15.3px). Measured in Chrome at 127.0.0.1: root 17px, self-check label 15.3px, one 53px footer row, no page-level overflow, and `main.app-main` as the only scrolling region besides the draft textarea.
3. **Footer.** The two loose sentences became one footer bar: a small stone mark, the tagline, and the non-official-service statement in a bordered pill. Both sentences keep their exact wording so the EN/繁/简 dictionary still applies; dark and high-contrast variants were added.
4. **Copy behaviour stated honestly.** The draft is a textarea, so its text can always be selected and copied. The page now says so, and explains that the Copy button additionally runs the self-check list and Fact Lock. Selection was deliberately not disabled: blocking it would break screen readers, magnification, and keyboard-only copying.

## 4. Corpus design — supervisor confirmation

**Date confirmed:** 2026-11-08
**Confirmed by:** Project supervisor (LAU Chaak Ming)

The supervisor confirmed that corpus exemplars can be:
- **Self-authored / synthetic** — written by the student or generated by a model and edited
- **Real cases** — actual student emails collected with consent, or public model emails from university writing centres

Both are acceptable. This resolves the concern about the corpus being entirely synthetic: the supervisor has explicitly endorsed it.

### 4.1 Target

- 40 human-verified exemplars (annotated with Swales move analysis + Biesenbach-Lucas e-politeness features)
- 60 synthetic input scenarios (for testing the Gatekeeper and lint, not as gold-standard exemplars)
- 10–15 public model emails from university writing centres (preferred provenance class)

### 4.2 Provenance separation

Synthetic data may serve as **input scenarios** (what the student types into the tool) but should be distinguished from **reference exemplars** (what the tool compares against). The circular-argument risk — a model trained on its own outputs — is mitigated by this separation.
